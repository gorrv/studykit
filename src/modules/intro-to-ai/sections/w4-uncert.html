Uncertainty in search

The two assumptions we have been making all along
Search algorithms — BFS, DFS, A* — typically assume:
  • The current state is known → "fully observable"
  • Actions always execute as intended → "deterministic"

These don't apply in many real-world applications:

  • Current state uncertain → "partially observable"
  • Results of actions uncertain → "stochastic"
You have seen these four words before
They are two of the seven dimensions from Week 1. Every algorithm in Weeks 1–3 quietly sat at the easy end of both. This week we drop one of them — actions become stochastic — and keep full observability. Dropping the other gives you a POMDP, which is a much harder object and not on this course.

Uncertainty in supervised learning

The setup
  • Training set: examples of input data (images, features) and output labels.
  • Models then predict labels in new examples.
Four places uncertainty enters
  • Training labels uncertain — was the human annotation done correctly?
  • Input data noisy — is this the real distance from the pedestrian to the car?
  • Model predictions uncertain — how confident are we in the model's prediction? Is this a cat or a dog? Will that pedestrian try to cross?
  • The world is uncertain — some things are stochastic and can never be predicted 100% accurately:
    • pedestrian movement prediction for autonomous vehicles;
    • text generation with language models — many different ways of completing "Mary crossed the road and then ______".
The fourth one is different in kind from the first three
Noisy labels, noisy inputs and an unconfident model are all problems you could in principle fix — annotate more carefully, buy a better sensor, train a bigger model. The world being stochastic is not fixable. No amount of data tells you which way a pedestrian will step, because they have not decided yet.

That is the kind of uncertainty MDPs are for. You do not try to eliminate it — you build it into the model as a probability distribution and then optimise the expected outcome.

The row that matters most
A plan is no longer a useful answer. If your actions can go wrong, a fixed sequence stops being valid the moment one of them does — and you would have to replan. A policy says what to do in every state, so it is still valid no matter where you end up. That single shift is what the rest of the week is about.

Exam-style takeaways

Key things to nail for Part 1
  1. Search's two assumptions — current state known (fully observable) and actions execute as intended (deterministic).
  2. When they break — uncertain current state gives a partially observable problem; uncertain action results give a stochastic one.
  3. MDPs relax exactly one: fully observable but stochastic.
  4. Four sources of uncertainty in supervised learning — uncertain training labels, noisy input data, uncertain model predictions, and a genuinely stochastic world.
  5. Be able to give the examples — annotation quality, sensor distance to a pedestrian, cat-or-dog confidence, pedestrian movement for AVs, language-model text completion.
  6. Irreducible vs reducible uncertainty — the first three could be improved with better data or models; a stochastic world cannot be, which is why it has to be modelled rather than removed.

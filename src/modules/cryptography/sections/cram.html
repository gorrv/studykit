Topic 01 · what security means

The two objects, as syntax

  • SKE = (KGen, Enc, Dec) over key space K, message space M, ciphertext space C.
  • sk ← KGen · c = Enc(sk, m) · m = Dec(sk, c). The arrow is there because key generation is random.
  • MAC = (KGen, Tag, Vf) with its own integrity key ik. τ = Tag(ik, m), and Vf returns 0 or 1.
  • Stating either triple says nothing about correctness or security. It is the shape, not the guarantee.

Correctness

  • c = Enc(sk, m) ⇒ m = Dec(sk, c). That is the whole obligation.
  • It is a statement about the scheme agreeing with itself, and is not a weak form of security.
  • The "do nothing" cipher is perfectly correct and perfectly useless.

The ladder, and what kills each rung

  • i key not recoverable → killed by the scheme that never uses the key.
  • ii messages not recoverable → killed by leaking the first letter.
  • iii partial messages not recoverable → killed by leaking statistics.
  • iv no further information inferable → the right intent, not yet formal.
  • v iv, even with chosen encryptions and decryptions → the one to aim at.

What a definition must say

  • What must not be possible — inferring further meaningful information about the messages.
  • Given what capabilities — observing only, or also encrypting and decrypting chosen inputs.
  • Change either half and it is a different claim. Nothing is "secure" without saying against whom.
  • This family of properties is confidentiality, or secrecy.

Breaking a shift cipher

  • A shift moves the letter counts along without changing them, so the counts identify the key.
  • Score each of the 26 shifts by χ² against English frequencies; the smallest wins.
  • Measured on a fixed passage: 10 letters → 83.7%, 20 → 98.6%, 30 → 100%.
  • Short messages are the only protection, and it runs out quickly.

The one-time pad

  • One uniform key letter per message letter, added letter by letter.
  • Perfect secrecy: for any message and any ciphertext there is exactly one key joining them, so every message stays exactly as likely as it was.
  • Not a computational claim — the information is absent, so no amount of work recovers it.
  • Costs: key as long as the message, never reusable, and no integrity at all.

Integrity

  • The assurance that what arrived is what was sent. A separate property from secrecy, achieved by separate means and a separate key.
  • The pad is the proof they are separate: Dec(k, c + d) = m + d. The attacker learns nothing and still chooses the message.
  • Engineering answers: indelible ink, no blank space, tamper-evident seals.
  • Cryptographic answer: a tag over the ciphertext under a second key, checked before decrypting.

Neither property covers these

  • Reordering, repeating, or reflecting valid ciphertexts and tags — each is genuine, so integrity is satisfied.
  • Delaying or denying delivery — generally network engineering rather than cryptography.
  • Every one of these needs its own sentence, with its own two halves.

The cast

  • Alice and Bob hold the keys.
  • Eve eavesdrops and nothing more — rung iv.
  • Charlie is active: chosen messages encrypted, chosen ciphertexts decrypted — rung v.
  • Eve is rarely a strong enough adversary to design against.
